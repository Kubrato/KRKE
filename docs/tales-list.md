Unique tales (only one country) will come later.

**Legend**

- ✅ = found and checked in a source (link below)
- ❓ = not found yet → the country expert should search
- ≈ = similar story, but scholars say it is only *related* to the type (not the same type)

**What we learned:**

- No tale type is confirmed in **all 4** countries yet. Most have 3.
- **Japan** fits well with the "magic and reward" tales (1–4).
- **Turkey** fits well with the long "palace and prince" tales (5–9).
- The very famous tales (10–12: Rapunzel, Sleeping Beauty, Snow White) are easy to find in Germany and Italy, but we found **no Japanese or Turkish versions yet**. They may be mostly European stories. That is also interesting: it can be a CQ ("Which tale types exist only in Europe?").
- **Wikidata is incomplete**: many German and Italian tales have an ATU code there, but almost no Japanese or Turkish tales do. This is a good point for our project: **our KG can add this missing knowledge.**

**Turkish catalogue table (checked):** Eberhard & Boratav, pp. 421–422, *Vergleichstafel nach Antti Aarne*, matches old Aarne numbers to Turkish TTV numbers. The authors list a match "only where a real correspondence seems to exist". Matches for our types: 408 → TTV 89 · 425 → 98 · 503 → **118** · 510 → **60** · 545B → 34 · 700 → **288** · 707 → 239 · 709 → **167** (+ Appendix A b) · 1640 → **330** (doesn't fit, see row 7) · 310 → Appendix C 12. **No match** for 480 and 410. Note: this table uses the old Aarne system (1928), where 510 is still one type (510A and 510B were not separate).

**How to read the catalogue's sources:** p. 423 lists the handwritten collections in Boratav's Folklore Archive in Ankara, e.g. "*Ankara 2*, Zeynep Dengi (30)" = collection name, collector, number of tales. So "*Ankara 32,3*" = collection Ankara 32, tale no. 3.

**To fill the ❓ gaps:**

- 🇹🇷 Turkish versions: look in the Eberhard & Boratav catalogue *Typen türkischer Volksmärchen* (1953). It has its own numbers (TTV), and many TTV types match ATU types. There is a digital copy at the University of Halle: https://opendata.uni-halle.de/handle/1981185920/36665
- 🇯🇵 Japanese versions: look in Hiroko Ikeda, *A Type and Motif Index of Japanese Folk-Literature* (1971), and in *The Yanagita Kunio Guide to the Japanese Folk Tale*.

## Ideas for unique Turkish tales (from the catalogue's appendix C, Mehmet Tuğrul collection)

- *Keloğlan und die Hocatochter* / Keloğlan and the Hoca's Daughter (appendix C 13, pp. 418–419): Keloğlan wants to marry the hoca's daughter; a lion takes his place, and on the way they see strange signs (a nightingale, a cypress, a dog, three pots) that are explained as signs of the end of the world.
- *Ayı kulak* / Bear's Ear (appendix C 3, p. 414): a half-bear, half-human son frightens the authorities in Bodrum and even the devils.
